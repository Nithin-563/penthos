To detect a cycle in a singly linked list, **Floyd's Tortoise and Hare algorithm** is a classic approach. It uses two pointers, one moving at twice the speed of the other, to determine if a cycle exists. Here's a breakdown of the algorithm, its correctness, and its comparison with other approaches.

---

### **Floyd's Tortoise-and-Hare Algorithm**

#### **How it works:**
- **Tortoise pointer** moves one step at a time.
- **Hare pointer** moves two steps at a time.
- If there is a cycle, the two pointers will eventually meet (i.e., point to the same node), indicating a cycle.
- If there is no cycle, the hare will reach the end of the list before the tortoise.

#### **Pseudocode:**
```python
def has_cycle(head):
    slow = head
    fast = head
    while fast and fast.next:
        slow = slow.next
        fast = fast.next.next
        if slow == fast:
            return True
    return False
```

---

### **Why it terminates and detects cycles correctly:**

1. **Termination:**
   - The algorithm terminates when either:
     - The **hare pointer** reaches the end of the list (i.e., `fast` or `fast.next` is `None`), or
     - The **tortoise and hare pointers meet** (i.e., `slow == fast`).
   - In the case of no cycle, the hare will eventually reach the end, and the loop will exit.
   - In the case of a cycle, the pointers will eventually meet, and the loop will exit.

2. **Cycle detection:**
   - If there is a cycle, the two pointers will eventually meet because they are moving at different speeds. This is a consequence of the **pigeonhole principle**.
   - The **meeting point** is guaranteed to be within the cycle, and the presence of a meeting point confirms the existence of a cycle.

---

### **Space Complexity Comparison:**

| Approach               | Space Complexity |
|-----------------------|------------------|
| **Floyd's algorithm** | **O(1)**         |
| **Hash-set approach** | **O(n)**         |

- **Floyd's algorithm** uses only two pointers, so it requires **constant space**.
- **Hash-set approach** stores all visited nodes in a hash set, which requires **O(n)** space in the worst case.

---

### **Time Complexity:**

| Approach               | Time Complexity |
|-----------------------|-----------------|
| **Floyd's algorithm** | **O(n)**        |
| **Hash-set approach** | **O(n)**        |

- Both approaches have **linear time complexity** in the worst case.
- **Floyd's algorithm** is more space-efficient and is preferred in environments with limited memory.

---

### **Summary:**

- **Floyd's Tortoise-and-Hare algorithm** is a **space-efficient** and **time-efficient** way to detect cycles in a singly linked list.
- It uses **O(1)** space and **O(n)** time.
- The **hash-set approach** is **easier to understand** and **more readable**, but it uses **O(n)** space.
- Floyd's algorithm is a **classic and elegant** solution that leverages the properties of linked lists and the pigeonhole principle.
