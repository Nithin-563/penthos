To detect a cycle in a singly linked list, **Floyd's Tortoise and Hare algorithm** (also known as the **Floyd's cycle-finding algorithm**) is a classic solution. Here's a breakdown:

---

### **Floyd's Algorithm: How It Works**
1. **Two pointers**: Use two pointers, `slow` (tortoise) and `fast` (hare).  
   - `slow` moves 1 step at a time.  
   - `fast` moves 2 steps at a time.  
2. **If a cycle exists**: The two pointers will eventually meet at some node.  
3. **If no cycle exists**: The `fast` pointer will reach the end of the list before the `slow` pointer.

---

### **Proof of Correctness**
- **Cycle Existence**:  
  Suppose the list has a cycle. Let:  
  - `D` = distance from head to the cycle entry point.  
  - `L` = length of the cycle.  
  The `slow` pointer takes `D + kL` steps to reach the cycle entry point (where `k` is an integer).  
  The `fast` pointer, moving twice as fast, takes `D + kL` steps to reach the same point.  
  Once in the cycle, the `fast` pointer laps the `slow` pointer after `L` steps, leading to a meeting.  

- **No Cycle**:  
  The `fast` pointer will reach the end of the list before the `slow` pointer, as the `fast` pointer moves twice as fast.  

**Key Insight**: The algorithm terminates because the `fast` pointer cannot exceed `2n` steps (where `n` is the number of nodes), and the `slow` pointer will either meet the `fast` pointer or reach the end.

---

### **Comparison with Hash-Set Approach**
| **Aspect**               | **Floyd's Algorithm**         | **Hash-Set Approach**         |
|--------------------------|-------------------------------|-------------------------------|
| **Space Complexity**     | O(1)                         | O(n)                         |
| **Time Complexity**      | O(n)                         | O(n)                         |
| **Implementation**       | Simple, no extra data structures | Requires storing all visited nodes |
| **Scalability**          | Better for large lists       | Worse for large lists         |

**Floyd's** is more space-efficient, but both have linear time complexity. The hash-set approach is easier to implement but uses O(n) memory.

---

### **Time Complexity**
- **Floyd's Algorithm**: O(n) time (worst case: `fast` pointer traverses 2n steps).  
- **Hash-Set Approach**: O(n) time (each node is visited once).  

Both algorithms have the same worst-case time complexity, but Floyd's is more efficient in terms of memory.
